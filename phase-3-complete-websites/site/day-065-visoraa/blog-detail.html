Essay · October 22, 2026 · By Lena Hart

On looking,
slowly.

An invitation to spend a little longer with an artwork—and to notice what your first impression left behind.

↓
A practice of attention

We are practiced at seeing quickly. We recognize, categorize, move on. In a gallery, however, an image can ask something different of us: not an answer, but a little time.

The first glance is only a doorway

Stand before a painting and the familiar mechanisms begin. Color registers, a form suggests itself, a title offers a clue. But stay for another minute. The edges may become less certain. A mark that seemed incidental starts to hold the composition. What looked like an empty field begins to feel charged.

Slow looking is not a test of expertise. It is a way of allowing the work to meet you without rushing to explain it. The questions are simple: What do I notice? What changes? What keeps drawing my eye back?

Attention makes room

Artists often spend weeks, months, or years refining a single decision. The viewer need not match that duration—but a few unhurried moments can reveal the care held inside a surface, a gesture, or a relationship between objects.

“Looking is not passive. It is a way of being in conversation.”

Next time you visit, choose one work. Let the room fall away for a moment. Stay until something shifts, however subtly. That shift is yours to take with you.
